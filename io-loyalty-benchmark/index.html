EAR Formula

EAR (%) = [(Gross Rewards − Annual Fee) / Annual Spend] × 100

Effective Annual Return. Modeled on three spend tiers, net of all fees. Rewards valued at face cash value unless otherwise noted.

Spending Tiers

  • Light — $12,000/yr (~$1,000/mo)
  • Mid — $36,000/yr (~$3,000/mo)
  • Heavy — $120,000/yr (~$10,000/mo)

Exchange Rate

3.5 PEN/USD used for all soles-denominated figures.

Redemption Friction Score (RFS)

  • Low — Auto statement credit or instant discount. No manual steps.
  • Medium — Manual redemption required; portal or category selection needed.
  • High — Complex transfer, staking lock-up, or crypto conversion required.

iO Lima, Peru
CCEAR 1.0%–4.7%+
Overview
Peruvian neobank backed by BCP. Single product: Visa credit card with Niveles iO loyalty program. 3 tiers based on monthly billing cycle (25th–24th).
Accumulation
1% base unlimited on all eligible spend. Experto/Maestro bonus: Cines 5% (cap S/10), Minimarket 5% (S/10), Streaming 5% (S/10), Moda 5% (S/20), Uber 10% (S/15), PedidosYa 15% (S/15). Maestro adds: Supermercados 3% (S/20) + Cashback Plus (1 category/mo): AI 20% (S/30) or Viajes/Grifos/Restaurantes 5% (S/30). All caps are independent, monthly reset. Annual fee: S/0.

Mid$36K2.2% (Maestro+AI)
Heavy$120K1.4% (Maestro+AI)
Base 1% only: 1.0% flat at all spend levels.
Redemption
Auto statement credit applied to card balance. RFS: Low. No minimum threshold. No expiration.
Engagement
App balance tracking. No gamification or streaks. No referral program (as of May 2026). Cashback Plus monthly category selection adds a loyalty touchpoint.
Known Failure Modes
  • MCC codes for bonus categories not publicly documented — qualifying transactions unclear

Framework
How loyalty programs are structured across the benchmark.
Every loyalty program in this benchmark falls into one of four structural archetypes. Most companies blend elements, but one archetype dominates their economics. iO sits in Archetype 2 — bonus category / tiered — closest structurally to Amex BCP, but with soles-denominated caps, zero fee, and auto statement credit.
1 · Flat-rate simple
Citi, Lemon, Venmo, PicPay, Freedom Unlimited
No category management. Scales linearly with spend. Best EAR for heavy spenders when rates are ≥2%. Weakness: no differentiation, no engagement hook.
2 · Bonus category / tiered ← iO
iO, Amex BCP, BofA, Nubank, KAST, Rappi
High EAR at moderate spend. Fixed caps mean EAR declines as total spend grows past cap threshold. Engagement through category awareness and tier progress.
3 · Subscription-gated
Robinhood, Revolut, N26, Crypto.com, Monzo Max
Monetizes loyalty directly. Negative EAR at low spend until break-even. Requires users to model ROI before subscribing — high friction at acquisition.
4 · Crypto-volatile
Gemini, Coinbase, Binance, Nexo, Ether.fi, KAST
Headline EAR is attractive but real value is crypto-price-dependent. High RFS. Not meaningfully comparable to fiat programs. Bear markets create churn and reputational risk.

iO position
What Niveles iO does well — and what it doesn't have yet.
Structural assessment as of May 2026. Compared against the 39 benchmarked programs.
Strengths
✅
No annual fee — only Citi, Chime, Venmo, PicPay offer meaningful earn without a fee. iO's S/0 fee is genuinely differentiated.
✅
No staking requirement — 6 crypto competitors require staking; iO has zero participation friction.
✅
No crypto volatility — rewards denominated in soles; real value is guaranteed regardless of market conditions.
✅
Lowest redemption friction (RFS: Low) — auto statement credit matches best-in-class (Nubank, Venmo, PicPay) and beats all traditional Peruvian banks.
✅
Highest light-spend EAR in LATAM non-crypto segment — 4.7% at $12K/yr beats Nubank Ultravioleta (0.72% light), Rappi (~4% but spend-mix dependent), and all Peruvian bank cards (~0.75%).
Gaps
❌
No gamification or missions — Tenpo, Rappi, Mercado Pago, Cash App all use challenge/mission mechanics to drive transaction frequency with near-zero incremental cost.
❌
No referral program — Nubank, PicPay, Rappi, Mercado Pago all have referral mechanics. Referral is typically the lowest-CAC acquisition channel for neobanks.
❌
No real-time progress tracking UX — users can't see tier progress, distance to next tier, or Cashback Plus selection reminder without manual calculation.
❌
No DC loyalty program — all major Peruvian banks and LATAM neobanks have debit earn. iO is CC-only; the DC segment is growing.
❌
No partner-funded merchant boost offers — Amex Offers, Chase Offers, BofA Deals, Cash App Boosts drive engagement at near-zero loyalty cost by shifting funding to merchants. iO has partner discounts but not cashback-format offers.
Recommendations
Five things iO should — and should not — replicate.
1
Do NOT replicate crypto-volatile rewards. LATAM users are increasingly crypto-aware, but EAR instability creates churn and support burden. iO's soles-denominated cashback is a structural advantage — protect it. Competitors with CRO/BNB/USDC earn cannot guarantee the real value iO delivers on every transaction.
2
Do replicate mission-based engagement (Tenpo model). Temporary spend missions are low-cost, high-engagement, and don't dilute base program economics. A mission like "earn 5% on restaurants this weekend" drives incremental spend without changing the permanent structure. Tenpo proves this works at scale in LATAM.
3
Do replicate referral mechanics (Nubank, Rappi, Mercado Pago). Cost-per-acquisition via referral is typically lower than paid channels and creates social proof loops. Referral rewards can be funded from new-customer lifetime value — the economics are almost always positive.
4
Watch the DC program gap. All major Peruvian banks (BCP, Interbank, BBVA) and LATAM neobanks (Mercado Pago, Tenpo) have debit earn. iO's CC-only model is viable as differentiation today, but the debit segment is growing. Not urgent, but worth tracking as a potential second product.
5
Avoid subscription-gated loyalty (Revolut, N26, Robinhood model). European subscription loyalty has high negative EAR at low spend and requires break-even analysis that confuses users. iO's zero-fee structure is genuinely differentiated in LATAM — adding a subscription tier risks undermining it without proportional engagement gain.
