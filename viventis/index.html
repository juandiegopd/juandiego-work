02 · The Business

How it works.

Viventis is not insurance. It is a health discount membership built on a DPO — Discount Provider Organization — structure. The company negotiates bulk discounts with pharmacy networks, labs, telemedicine providers, and dental and vision partners. Employers pay a monthly subscription per enrolled employee. Members receive a digital card that unlocks those discounts at point of service. No claims. No underwriting. No open enrollment. No regulatory complexity.

What it does not cover

Hospitalizations, surgeries, specialist visits, emergency room, and anything catastrophic. Viventis covers routine, high-frequency healthcare. For a healthy 30-year-old worker, this addresses roughly 80% of their actual annual healthcare interactions.

This clarity is a sales asset, not a liability. Workers who understand exactly what the card does — and what it does not — trust it more and use it more.
Revenue model

Pure subscription. Employer pays monthly per enrolled employee. No claims. No usage-based variance. Highly predictable cash flow.

Plan structure

Individual, couple, and family tiers. Employer typically pays individual plan. Plan mix compounds ARPU above the $39 base — blended revenue per employer is higher.

Gross margin est.

65–70%, benchmarked against GoodRx and comparable DPO-based membership companies. Structural: no claims to pay, low variable cost per member.

On May 18, 2026, the White House expanded TrumpRx.gov to 600+ generic drugs via partnerships with Cost Plus Drugs, Amazon Pharmacy, and GoodRx. The US government has confirmed at the highest level that the uninsured cash-pay drug market is real and politically important.

The problem Viventis is solving just got validated from the White House.

What TrumpRx also reveals: a DTC, English-only, tech-navigation-required platform reaches only 7% of potential Rx users. The target customer — an uninsured Hispanic construction worker — will not find TrumpRx.gov on their own. They need an employer to bring the solution to them. That is exactly what Viventis does.
Watch closely
GoodRx Employer Direct
In February 2026, GoodRx launched Employer Direct — allowing employers to subsidize high-cost medications like GLP-1s at the pharmacy counter. First employer pilot: Hy-Vee. 2025 revenue: $796.9M.

GoodRx is entering the employer channel. This is the real competitive move to monitor.

The distinction: GoodRx is targeting employers to solve a high-cost medication problem for workers who already have benefits. Viventis is solving a no-benefits problem for workers who have nothing. Different buyer problem, different conversation — but the channel overlap will grow as GoodRx scales employer relationships.

TrumpRx is a tool. GoodRx is a pharmacy platform moving upmarket. Viventis is a managed health access benefit that shows up at the worksite, speaks Spanish, and proves value to the employer every month. None of the large players are building that.

06 · First 30 Days

Understand before building anything.

The business has 2,500 members. That is not a commercial engine — it is a proof of concept. The first 30 days are about understanding what is actually working before touching it.

Phase 01
Week 1
Days 1–7
Build the truth dashboard

07 · The Growth Strategy

Build the engine. Then scale it.

The commercial engine is built in four phases. Each phase unlocks the next. Nothing scales before the previous layer is proven.

Phase 01
Direct
Days 1–90
Operator-led direct sales
Sell directly without intermediaries. The goal is not revenue — it is learning. Every conversation surfaces the real objection, the real buying trigger, the real ICP. By Day 90: pitch is proven, activation playbook designed, first case studies exist. This phase is the foundation everything else is built on.
Phase 02
Brokers
Months 3–9
Broker-assisted co-selling
Benefits brokers are the outsourced benefits brain for SMB employers. 70–90% of SMB supplemental health benefit purchases go through a broker. Target bilingual brokers and brokers serving construction, hospitality, food service, and agriculture employers in Texas, Florida, and California. Commission: $75 per activated employee after 60 days + 10% renewal. Paying on activation aligns broker incentives with actual usage.

08 · The Operator

The same problem. Different market.

Every company I have joined has had the same underlying structure: product-market fit confirmed, commercial engine missing or broken. The job has always been the same — understand what is actually working, build the playbook around it, scale it. I have done this three times. The market changes. The motion does not.

Favo · 2020–2024
Head of New Business & Growth
B2B e-commerce · Peru & Brazil · 25× growth
When Favo's costs spiraled, I took over Sales and CX — a 55-person team — and found a structural bleed hiding in plain sight. A previous head of sales had raised commission caps for top wholesalers to 15%. Favo was losing money on 50% of its revenue. Nobody had modeled it clearly.

I built a per-customer P&L allocating logistics cost by SKU, by drop, by distribution method. Then I called each one of the 50 major wholesalers personally — starting from my desk to understand their business model — and then went to visit them directly: their operations, their distribution points, and in some cases their homes. The field work mattered. What you understand about how someone actually runs their business is different from what they tell you on a call. Once I had the full picture, I presented the new commission structure to all 50 wholesalers at once. I redesigned the entire model: eliminated the commission and gave them cost-price access instead, consolidated logistics around bigger trucks, optimized routes. Logistics cost dropped from ~14% to under 6%. Contribution margin went from -6% to +6% in four months — a 12-point swing on 50% of company revenue. That result extended Favo's runway by over a year.

Separately: built the direct-to-bodega model from zero. Went to the bodegas directly — selling, pitching, absorbing objections — before writing a single playbook. Hired 15+ reps, onboarded 3,500+ bodegas, hit $50K MRR in three months.
Transfer to Viventis: Find the 3 employers who love the product most. Understand exactly why. Build the commercial model around them — not around assumptions. Go to the worksite before building the system.
